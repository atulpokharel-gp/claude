## Problem Statement

Four cold-inbound MSP leads have contacted the founder without converting. The founder originally framed the problem as "the landing page is confusing and doesn't look premium." The real problem, surfaced in this session, is twofold:

1. **Distribution format gap.** The scanner already ships a daily-background-scan capability via `scanner/rmm/install-windows.ps1` (Task Scheduler, 2h random delay) and `install-unix.sh` (launchd / systemd timer / cron). But the signed-in `ConnectLaptops` screen in `/app/clients/[id]` surfaces only the bare `zinloop --upload …` CLI, not the RMM-ready one-liner. Real MSPs deploy via signed MSI packages pushed through NinjaOne / Datto RMM / N-able / ConnectWise Automate — not curl-pipe commands. The capability exists; the delivery format does not.
2. **Observation gap.** The founder has never watched any of the four cold inbounds attempt the current flow unassisted. Every diagnosis of "where it breaks" is self-generated.

## Demand Evidence

Four cold inbounds across three unrelated verticals, received without outbound effort:
- **smart365guide** — the founder's own domain. Internal test tenant, not external demand. Noted for honesty.
- **Cytible** — a biomedical-literature product the founder is connected to (visible as `mcp__claude_ai_cytible__*` in this session's tool list). Warm connection, not independent demand.
- **Fleetpand** — fleet management vertical. Independent third party. Status of relationship not yet confirmed in detail; qualified as "cold inbound."
- **NRG** — unknown vertical. Independent third party. Same qualification.

Shared feedback across the leads: "we need a simple way to run as a background task."

Behavioral signal: zero of the four has actually run a scan on real client laptops. The current conversion funnel is **personal 1:1 walkthroughs** with each lead by the founder.

Honest demand grading: 2 real cold inbounds (Fleetpand, NRG) across 2 verticals is a weak-but-genuine signal. Enough to justify a concierge wedge for one named MSP, not enough to justify a full funnel rebuild without validation first.

## Status Quo

**For the leads:** Cross-platform CLI exists; RMM install scripts exist in `scanner/rmm/`; signed MSI / `.pkg` does NOT exist. Leads see `curl … | iex` on GitHub and interpret this as "manual CLI tool" rather than "RMM-deployable scheduled scanner."

**For the founder:** Every lead gets a personal walkthrough. No lead has deployed independently. No lead has been watched trying the flow unassisted.

**For the market:** MSPs today handle AI connector risk by (a) ignoring it, (b) running occasional ad-hoc greps for `.mcp.json` / `claude_desktop_config.json`, or (c) relying on their existing EDR / DLP vendors who don't understand MCP-shaped risk. There is no established competing product targeting MSP channels for MCP/AI-connector security yet. Zinloop's wedge is the signed-MSI + per-RMM-template bundle, not the risk-detection rules (which also exist but are not currently differentiated in the market because the market hasn't noticed yet).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user — specific human:** The MSP owner-operator or senior technician at a 5-50 technician MSP serving mid-market clients (50-500 seats each) who is already seeing clients install Claude Desktop / Cursor / Windsurf and is being asked by those clients "are we okay on AI?" They run NinjaOne, Datto RMM, N-able, or ConnectWise Automate and expect to deploy anything new via an MSI they can push through their RMM's script library. They do not deploy anything that requires per-laptop manual steps.

**Narrowest wedge:** A concierge-mode deployment for ONE named MSP (Fleetpand or NRG). Signed Windows MSI with silent-install args, signed macOS `.pkg` with the same, per-RMM script-library snippet for that MSP's specific RMM, upload token baked in. Target: 10+ real client laptops uploading within 48 hours of handoff, founder watches at least two installs live.

  - **Apple Developer Program**: $99/yr, 1-2 business days for individual enrollment; **business/organization enrollment with D-U-N-S** typically takes several business days to 2+ weeks (per `docs/signing.md`, Zinloop needs the organization path, not individual).

- Vercel deployment pipeline exists (per `docs/vercel.md`); no new deploy infra required for the funnel work.

## Premises

Five premises agreed by the founder; Premise 2 revised mid-session after cross-model challenge.

1. **Demand is real.** 2 unambiguous cold inbounds across 2 verticals (Fleetpand, NRG), plus 2 warm connections (smart365guide = own domain, Cytible = connected product), is genuine external signal. Enough to justify a concierge wedge; not enough to justify polish-first.
2. **The gap is distribution format, not UX exposure.** (Revised from "UX exposure" after Phase 3.5 challenge.) The scheduling capability exists in `install-windows.ps1` / `install-unix.sh`. What is missing: signed MSI / `.pkg`, per-RMM script-library templates for the top 4 RMMs, and a console surface that hands the MSP the right installer + token in their RMM's native format. The design must produce the installer and templates, not just a prettier page.
3. **Session scope is the acquisition path only.** Landing → `/free-scan` sign-up → add first client → copy-paste RMM installer → first scan uploaded. Explicitly out of scope: client detail page redesign, M365 panel changes, Zipax sub-product, console navigation, report rendering.
4. **Premium visual pass only ships bundled with the UX fix.** No standalone "redesign the site" branch. A beautiful landing routing to a bare CLI converts zero.
5. **Validation is mandatory.** Within 7 days of shipping, the founder watches at least one of {Fleetpand, NRG} try the real flow unassisted on a 20-min screenshare. If none agrees to screenshare, that itself is signal and the next session is customer discovery, not more design.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent reviewer saw the problem statement, Q2/Q4/Q5 answers, and premises without the conversation. Its cold read:

- **Steelman of the business:** Not a scanner, a **compliance / liability attestation** MSPs can bill as a line item. The scanner is ingest; the monthly PDF / portal artifact handed to a mid-market CFO is the sellable thing. The $5-8/seat resale economics only make sense if the deliverable is attestation, not scan output.
- **Key insight:** Q5 — "founder has never watched a lead" — is the entire session in one line. Everything downstream is imagination until a lead screen-shares.
- **Challenged premise:** Originally Premise 2 ("gap is UX exposure"). Reviewer argued the gap is distribution format: signed MSI + per-RMM script-library entries. Premise 2 was revised to reflect this.
- **48h prototype suggestion:** Skip the landing rebuild entirely for now. Build a concierge MSP tenant for one named lead: signed MSI + signed `.pkg`, per-RMM one-pager for THAT MSP's RMM, auto-generated PDF per client, Loom walkthrough. Success = 10+ real laptops in 48h and ≥2 watched installs.

The reviewer's concierge path was added as a formal alternative in Phase 4 rather than overriding the original scope.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Full acquisition flow + signed installer (XL)
End-to-end rebuild in one sprint: landing + `/free-scan` + install wizard + first-scan proof, bundled with signed MSI, signed `.pkg`, and ready-made RMM script templates for NinjaOne / Datto / N-able / ConnectWise Automate. 2-3 weeks. Rejected as premature — biggest "polishing the wrong flow" risk, since no lead has been watched yet. Reusable artifacts (signed installer, RMM templates) are valuable but ship inside a flow that is unvalidated.

### Approach B: Concierge wedge for one named MSP (M)
One week. Pick Fleetpand or NRG, ask what RMM they run, build only what unblocks THEM. Signed installer in their RMM's native format, PDF report per client, Loom walkthrough. No public funnel work. Rejected as sole path because it does not address the stated original ambition (clean premium funnel). But its validation value is high — the first week should look like this.

### Approach C: Hybrid — concierge first, funnel second (XL overall) — RECOMMENDED
Week 1 is Approach B scope. Week 2-3 is Approach A scope, informed by what week 1 revealed. The signed installer + RMM template work does double duty — concierge in week 1, funnel in week 2. Discipline hazard: must not start week 2 until week 1 lands a real scan.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C (Hybrid).** Rationale:

- Preserves the founder's stated ambition (premium acquisition flow) without inverting the Q5 insight.
- Orders validation before polish — the week-2 funnel design is informed by one watched real install rather than imagination.
- The hardest shared work (code-signing cert procurement, signed MSI, signed `.pkg`, RMM templates) serves both weeks; nothing is throwaway.
- A natural stop-point exists at end of week 1 — if the concierge MSP ghosts and no scan happens, week 2 is deferred and the next session is discovery, not polish.

8. Record one Loom (10-15 min) showing: "You're an MSP. You received this MSI. Here's how it goes into your RMM. Here's what shows up. Here's a sample of the monthly attestation PDF you'll hand your client's CFO (shown as a static Pages/Docs sample for Week 1; auto-generated in Week 2)." Share this plus the installer with the target MSP.

Watch ≥**2 per-laptop installs live** over screenshare with the target MSP — meaning the RMM operator at the MSP triggers the RMM deployment and you watch at least two individual client laptops receive, install, and successfully upload a scan during that same screenshare. One batched RMM-deployment event that fires on 50 laptops in the background does NOT count as "2 installs"; the point of this criterion is to watch per-laptop friction (SmartScreen prompts, admin elevation UAC prompts, Gatekeeper, antivirus false positives, token-file permission errors), which is only visible per laptop. Capture friction points verbatim.

SignPath is NOT used — this plan was corrected during /plan-eng-review after surface-check found the existing wiring. - Signing: reuse existing Apple Developer ID + API-key signing already wired in `release.yml:sign-macos` and documented in `docs/signing.md`.
